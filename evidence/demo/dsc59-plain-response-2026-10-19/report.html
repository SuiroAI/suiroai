What determines the order

Each order covers expected recorded demand through supplier lead time plus the seven-day review period, adds the supplied safety stock, and subtracts usable stock and deliveries due within that window. Positive shortfalls are rounded up to the supplied minimum and order multiple. Planning windows range from 8 to 14 days. Zero orders mean this rule finds sufficient supply under the stated assumptions; they do not mean zero demand.

Preparation and evidence that affect interpretation

  • Transactions: 17,406 rows become 17,364 after removing 42 exact duplicates. Transaction IDs are then unique. Status labels are trimmed and lowercased. Eight zero/negative-quantity rows are excluded without converting their signs; they lack sufficient evidence to identify valid stock returns.
  • Demand target: 15,142 positive completed, shipped or returned rows, totaling 32,198 product units. Cancelled orders (851 rows) and processing orders (1,364 rows) are excluded. Returned orders count as historical gross stock use. Positive returned quantities are not signed credits. There is no receipt date, return-to-stock status or usable recovery amount, so returns never add to usable stock. Another 271 rows have a return flag outside Returned status; the sensitivity excludes both categories.
  • All 336 stock keys, policy keys and historical product/store keys agree, without join multiplication. Product names have 135 missing values but one consistent nonmissing name per Product_ID; these names are used. Sales transaction Inventory_Level is not a dated usable stock snapshot and is never used for current supply.
  • The stock snapshot and policy effective date are both November 30, 2025. All stock and policy quantities are present and nonnegative, with positive lead times, minimums and multiples. All units are product_unit; a product described as a box or pack remains one product unit, not its inner pieces.
  • All 126 unique inbound lines were ordered by the cutoff and are due after it. Their 927 units agree exactly with On_Order_Qty at every key. The ledger declares itself complete. Missing ledger rows therefore support zero inbound quantities, independently of missing sales dates. These are known planned deliveries, not future sales outcomes. Arrivals are assumed usable at the start of their due date; delays would increase shortage risk.

Missing dates are unknown, and the forecasts are conditional

The full file contains some activity on every one of 1,430 calendar dates, but individual stores have transactions on only 638–1,080 dates. This establishes file activity, not complete store or product coverage. No daily grid is created, no missing sales date is filled with zero, and absence is not claimed to be an observed zero-sale day.

The event-rate baseline divides quantities after the first recorded event by elapsed days from that first event through the forecast cutoff, separately for each product/store. This uses inter-event and trailing waiting time; it does not equate days without rows with measured zero demand. It does assume stable capture and comparable future trading exposure. The recent-year candidate applies the same calculation to the preceding 365 days. The pooled candidate blends that rate with product and store marginal shares from the same window, with weight equal to observed events minus one against 12 pooled pseudo-events. Sparse undefined estimates fall back to that pooled rate. No fitted model uses price, personal data, transaction Inventory_Level or future sales.

Evaluation sums actual recorded events in each future window; empty event sums are zero recorded units, not verified zero customer demand. The time denominators and observed volumes cannot distinguish closures, missing capture, unavailable products, or stockouts. As a result, estimated demand can be understated; zero orders are conditional recommendations. We do not claim historical inventory reconstruction or lost-sales recovery.

Order statuses and return flags have no historical knowledge timestamps. Rolling cutoffs prevent future order dates from entering training, but a row’s supplied final status may have been updated later. These are retrospective record-based comparisons rather than fully reconstructed live forecasts. Current policy is used to calculate current orders, not asserted to have applied historically. December 2025 outcomes remain withheld. Seasonal holiday uplift is not validated by the selected rate model, and no December 2025 accuracy claim is made.

The ±25% demand cases are illustrative stress tests, not confidence bounds. The missing-date case multiplies each store’s rate by 1,430 divided by its observed transaction-date count (1.32–2.24 times), representing the hypothetical case where all absent store dates hid typical active-date activity. It is neither a correction nor an upper bound: absent dates may have been closures, and even observed dates may be incomplete. This scenario tests consequences without inserting synthetic daily sales observations. Sensitivity does not validate any individual zero order.

Safety stocks total 1,544 units across the supplied policy, and pack sizes reach 10 units. These rules can produce 20-unit orders even when forecast consumption is below two units. They explain why the aggregate list moves little under ±25% demand changes. Supplied safety stocks are used directly; there is no supported service target or cost basis to optimize them.

Recommended actions and costs

Review flagged pairs first for transfers or faster supply. Confirm usable stock, supplier timing, and the synthetic policy settings before operational purchasing; review the list weekly as the supplied policy specifies. Only inbound due within each protection window reduces the new order, and each delivery is counted once. Early shortage screening follows daily expected use and scheduled arrivals through the new order’s lead time; it cannot capture random purchase timing or delivery disruption.

For a weekly run over 336 product/store combinations, ongoing cost cannot yet be estimated in USD: deployment, metered compute or model usage and applicable rates are unavailable. The cost of this analysis run is separately unavailable because billable usage and applicable pricing were not exposed. Neither statement implies zero cost. Procurement spend is also unsupported: supplier purchase prices and a consistent currency basis were not supplied.
