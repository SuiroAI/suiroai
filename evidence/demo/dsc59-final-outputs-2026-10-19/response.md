As of November 30, 2025, the illustrative restocking list calls for **888 product units across 113 product/store pairs**, with **zero orders for 223 pairs**. The CSV covers all 28 products at all 12 stores. Zero orders mean available stock plus scheduled receipts meet the modeled target; they do not mean zero demand or guaranteed availability.

The inventory, inbound and ordering-policy files explicitly identify their contents as synthetic. These quantities therefore illustrate applying those supplied inputs to the released sales history; they are not evidence of actual store inventory needs. The sales file is a released development partition, whose coverage relative to the original customer dataset is not established. No scaling to the whole business is supported.

Important findings:

- Usable stock totals 2,869 units. The complete open-order ledger contains 126 unique lines totaling 927 units, exactly matching inventory on-order quantities for every product/store pair. All receipts are ordered by November 30 and due December 1–7; all fall within their applicable protection horizons. They are credited once, without also subtracting the inventory on-order field.
- LON-01 has the largest order total, 130 units, followed by MAN-01 at 127 and SYD-01 at 100. For example, AeroBook 14 Laptop at LON-01 requires 20 units: zero usable stock or receipts, expected protection-period demand of 2.2374 units, safety stock of 10, and a 10-unit order multiple.
- A daily expected-flow calculation flags 52 pairs with a possible shortage before a new order arrives. These need receipt confirmation or transfer/expedite review; placing the listed normal-lead-time order cannot resolve an earlier shortage. The flags are based on mean demand, not stockout probabilities.

I removed 42 exact duplicate rows from 17,406 transactions; remaining transaction IDs are unique. Order_ID is unsuitable as a deduplication key: 2,861 order IDs repeat, and 2,860 of these span multiple dates and customers. Distinct transaction lines were retained; order counts and customer-level conclusions are not used. Status capitalization was normalized. Eight nonpositive quantities were excluded from demand estimation; no sign correction was invented. Demand uses positive quantities with Completed, Shipped or Returned status: 15,142 lines and 32,198 units. Cancelled and Processing lines are excluded because fulfillment is unestablished. Positive returned quantities measure prior gross demand; they are neither subtracted as negative sales nor added to usable stock. Product names map consistently to product IDs, allowing missing names to be recovered.

The selected forecast uses the latest 365 calendar days, with each product/store rate blended toward its product-by-store pooled rate using 60 days of equivalent weight. A pooled December seasonal factor of 1.455, estimated solely from 2022–2024, adjusts the rate. Six alternatives were compared at 11 month-end origins from December 2024 through October 2025, predicting the next 14 days. The selected method had the lowest root mean squared error, 1.90 units per pair/horizon, versus 2.04 for a 90-day average and 2.19 for predicting no recorded units. The gain over the unseasonal blended annual model, 1.91, is small. These comparisons reuse the supplied historical status fields; absent status-change timestamps prevent a true reconstruction of what was known at each origin.

For each pair, the protection period is supplier lead time plus the seven-day review period: December 1 through December 8–14. Target stock equals expected demand over that period plus supplied safety stock, rounded up to a whole unit. Subtract usable on-hand stock and receipts due within that period; floor the shortage at zero. A positive shortage is increased to the minimum order and rounded up to the order multiple. Policy safety stock is used directly, without claiming an achieved service level. Usable stock is assumed available for this demand; reservations and current backorders are not supplied. Transaction-level Inventory_Level is not used as a dated stock snapshot, and Delivery_Days is not treated as supplier lead time.

Limitations change how to use the list:

- **Sparse records and unknown reporting coverage:** transactions occur on every one of the 1,430 network calendar dates, but individual stores have records on only 638–1,080 dates; 14 pairs have no transactions in the latest 90 days. Missing store/product dates remain unobserved, with no synthetic daily zero-demand observations inserted. Rates divide recorded units by calendar exposure, conditional on the extract capturing relevant movements across that period. Closures, incomplete capture and lost sales cannot be separated. The annual pooled estimate avoids treating recent silence as proof of zero demand, but cannot recover unrecorded demand. A hypothetical doubling of demand raises orders to 957 units; this is a scenario, not an estimated capture rate.
- **Large forecast uncertainty:** 63.9% of historical pair/horizons have no recorded eligible units. Aggregate absolute forecast error is 120.9% of recorded units, and the selected model underpredicts the historical total by 8.6%. The 90th percentile absolute pair/horizon error is 2.46 units. These are retrospective diagnostics, not prediction intervals or December accuracy claims. December 2025 sales outcomes were not accessed. No historical inventory availability is supplied, so censored demand and realized stockout performance cannot be estimated.
- **Policy and receipts strongly influence orders:** demand at minus/plus 25% produces 879/902 units; these are sensitivities, not confidence bounds. Excluding all returned or return-flagged sales removes 3,589 historical units and produces 884 order units, changing four pairs. If none of the inbound stock arrives, orders rise to 1,295 units across 164 pairs, changing 66 recommendations. Pack rounding can turn a small demand estimate above a safety-stock boundary into a substantial order. Thus modest demand sensitivity does not establish forecast precision.
- **Timing and economic assumptions:** receipts are usable at the start of their due date; new orders arrive after the stated lead time, treated as calendar days. Future due dates are known operational inputs, not future sales observations. No receipt-status history establishes delivery reliability. No budget, holding cost, margin objective or capacity limits support claiming that this list is economically optimal.
