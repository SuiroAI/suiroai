# Internal analytical record

## Scope and source authority
Only the four approved released CSVs were read. They were read in full. Their byte hashes and row counts are in audit.json. The sales file is the released development partition, not its upstream original. No December outcomes or other source files were accessed. The task is a buying decision using the November 30, 2025 snapshot, for a December 1–7 review cycle. This is not a recommendation using current October 2026 inventory. All three operational files explicitly identify their provenance as synthetic, so the output is an illustrative, conditional plan.

## Preparation and measurement
The sales grain is a transaction line. Remove 42 identical rows, retaining all distinct order lines; repeated order IDs are not duplicate transactions. Transaction IDs are unique after that operation. Normalize status case. Exclude eight zero or negative quantities; no signed-return interpretation is supported, since these occur on completed, shipped and processing rows. Positive completed, shipped and returned lines measure gross fulfilled units assigned to the order date. All sales channels use their assigned store, with no claim that these were physical walk-in sales.

Returned orders represent an original outward movement; do not subtract them from gross fulfillment or add them to usable inventory. Return dates and resale suitability are unknown. Flags also occur outside the returned status. Current usable stock is already supplied separately. Cancelled and processing orders are excluded from fulfilled history. Final recorded statuses are not time-stamped as changes, so historical evaluation is a frozen-extract simulation, not proof of what a live system could have known. Features use only dated orders before each forecast origin; customer data, sales-row inventory levels and outcome-related fields are not forecast inputs.

Every network calendar day from January 1, 2022 through November 30, 2025 has at least one transaction, but individual stores and products have many absent dates. Fill missing cells only to measure **recorded fulfilled units on these network-observed days**. This is conditional ledger-event aggregation, not evidence of complete transaction capture, open stores, full product availability, or zero underlying demand. No dates outside that observation span are filled. Without historical stock and availability, latent demand and missed sales cannot be estimated. Persistent missing capture or stockouts would bias these rates down; therefore check availability and flagged shortages before placing actual orders. All 336 policy keys match both inventory keys and sales keys. All 28 product names have a single nonmissing spelling; missing names are filled using that verified mapping.

## Forecast comparison
analysis.py is the reproducible non-LLM implementation. Daily matrix: 1,430 dates by 336 products at stores, approximately 3.8 MB of numeric values. Compare five local rolling means (4, 8, 13, 26 and 52 weeks), three pooled rates (8, 13 and 26 weeks), and one pooled 13-week rate with calendar-month adjustment.

Pooling uses recent product-level units per calendar day and allocates them with prior 52-week product-specific store shares, shrunk by 30 units toward the overall store mix. The seasonal factor uses only earlier calendar years, with 60 pseudo-days of shrinkage toward the historical daily mean. The forecast month is the month four days after origin. These fixed smoothing constants were not optimized on evaluation outcomes.

Sunday origins run January 5 through November 16, 2025. At each origin, all history ends at origin, and actual observations for every tested horizon end by November 30. January–August origins select the model by mean absolute unit error over supplier lead time plus seven review days. The seasonal pooled 13-week model wins that selection. September–November contains 11 untouched evaluation origins, on exactly the same 336 keys and targets for all methods. Both seven-day and lead-plus-review forecasts are reported in model_evaluation.csv. Current lead times are used only to define illustrative forecast horizons, not to pretend historical stock policies were observed.

Against the simple 13-week local mean, the chosen model has less than 1% lower lead-plus-review absolute error and about 1% higher weekly absolute error. Neither difference is meaningful. It has somewhat lower squared error; do not advertise a single-metric win or superiority to the customer's actual process. The customer supplied a recent-months heuristic without a window, status rule or order-quantity calculation. The 13-week mean is a clearly labelled plausible baseline, not a reconstruction of that process. The selected model is retained because it was selected on the earlier development period and shares information for sparse series while permitting a conservative historical month adjustment. No measured benefit of improved purchasing, stockout reduction or profitability is claimed.

The 10,000 paired-origin bootstrap comparisons and exact diagnostics are in audit.json. Adjacent protection horizons overlap, and there are only 11 evaluation origins; these intervals are descriptive and may understate longer-run uncertainty. Many pair-level outcomes are zero and the upper residual tail is large relative to daily rates. No December outcome has been evaluated, and November/December seasonal conditions may differ from the evaluation average.

## Ordering calculation and timing
For each key, target = forecast daily gross fulfillment × (lead days + seven review days) + supplied safety stock. Subtract usable on-hand and eligible incoming units, then floor the gap at zero. For positive gaps only, apply minimum order and round upward to the order multiple. These rules are the supplied buying constraints, not the customer's historical procedure. Supplier lead times are 1–7 days. All 126 unique incoming lines were ordered by November 30 and are due December 1–7; all are inside every applicable protection horizon. Their sums exactly match all 336 on-order stock balances, with the ledger marked complete. There is no incoming double count. No paid orders were submitted.

The timing flag projects daily consumption and adds scheduled receipts at the start of their due day. Flag any negative balance strictly before the normal new-order arrival day, assuming orders placed after the November 30 snapshot arrive at the start of the lead-time day. The 52 flags indicate potential transfer or expedited-delivery needs, not certain stockouts. Dates are daily, and supplier delays and within-day demand are unmodelled.

The ±25% sales scenarios change total orders to 880–904 units versus 888 centrally. These are sensitivity scenarios, not probability intervals; minimum orders and safety stock make total buying insensitive to modest forecast changes while store-level shortage risk remains. Alternative target treatments exclude all return-flagged rows or include processing orders, giving 886 and 890 units. These sensitivities do not resolve unobserved lost sales. A firm service probability, optimal safety stock, budget allocation, shelf capacity, transfers and reorder profitability cannot be estimated from these files. Existing safety stock is honored rather than invented. The zero-order rows are supported arithmetic under these conditions, not claims of zero future demand.
