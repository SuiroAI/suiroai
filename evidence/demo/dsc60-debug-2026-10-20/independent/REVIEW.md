## Diagnosis: measured findings versus explanations

**P1: selection, justification and success criteria diverged.** Producer selects minimum seven-day MAE and then explains keeping the seasonal model using total bias, RMSE and holiday-buying judgment despite later MAE deterioration. Exact final response honestly discloses the larger individual errors, so an unsupported superiority assertion is not present in that response. Nonetheless these diagnostics cannot retrospectively make the primary performance criterion pass. No measurable customer quantity-setting procedure or predeclared meaningful improvement criterion was supplied. The recent-months description permits a labelled simple interpretation, not an exact incumbent replica.

**P1: sparse pair-level target and mean/MAE tension explain the observed reversal more directly than absent seasonality.** Only 17,018 of 480,480 pair-calendar-days have any record (3.54%). Positive noncancelled target rows occupy 16,183 pair-days. Weekly zero-target share is 79.12% in selection and 73.14% later. Later seasonal uplift adds 305.50 absolute-error units on zero targets, while saving only 148.43 on positive targets: net error increases 157.06 units. Its aggregate bias improves from baseline −24.42% to −1.45%, while pair-level MAE worsens. These are compatible outcomes. Mean-rate estimation is not optimized for a median-oriented absolute-error objective in intermittent data. This does not prove zero forecasting is a useful buying policy, nor authorize selecting one post hoc.

The aggregate seasonal rise itself persists: July–September versus October–November recorded units/day are 20.18→28.92 (2022), 23.61→30.41 (2023), 20.65→30.54 (2024), and 24.54→35.44 (2025). Therefore a blanket claim that seasonality failed to generalize is unsupported. Global monthly uplift can accurately describe network totals while failing the local primary error metric. Product/store factorization ignores interactions and fixed 50% pooling is a heuristic; whether alternate shrinkage or distributions improve future performance is a hypothesis, not established by this audit. Only three completed years support monthly factors; local seasonal stability remains uncertain.

**P1: target and capture are conditional.** Of 17,406 released rows, 42 exact duplicates leave 17,364 records. Normalized statuses include completed 11,130, shipped 2,479, returned 1,540, processing 1,364, cancelled 851; eight records have nonpositive units and 1,811 have return flag Yes. Including positive processing/returned units and excluding cancelled units defines gross recorded noncancelled orders, not completed demand, net consumption or known lost demand. Final status updates have no known availability time. Returned stock usability and return timing are unknown. All network dates populated does not establish complete capture for each key; latest first pair appearance is 2023-03-21. Zero-fill after first appearance is admissible only as explicitly conditional recorded-order analysis. It cannot certify true zero demand or stockout exposure. State sensitivity to treatment rather than assert complete sales capture.

**P1: forecast and ordering horizons differ.** Every policy uses 8–14 protection days (lead plus seven-day review), while primary selection uses seven days. Counts for 8 through 14 days are 50,44,50,46,38,49,59 keys. Fourteen-day diagnostics approximate only the longest coverage; per-policy horizon evaluation is warranted as a predeclared secondary diagnostic or a prospectively chosen primary metric. Do not switch to it because its results look better. Historical order/stock sequences and costs are unavailable, so forecast errors cannot establish realized service, purchasing savings or optimal replenishment.

Source-based order arithmetic and table agreement address a separate question. The recorded audits support 885 units /112 positive rows /224 zero rows for the final take; this review independently reconstructs forecasting metrics, and does not relabel prior arithmetic passes as superiority evidence.
