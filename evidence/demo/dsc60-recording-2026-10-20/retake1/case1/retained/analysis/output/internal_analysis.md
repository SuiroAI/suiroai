# Decision, evidence and limits

This is a November 30, 2025 development investigation using only the four released files. The business deliverable is the order list and deterministic weekly program. No report, dashboard or HTML narrative was built, as explicitly requested. The Data index, product-business-analysis, source-quality and notebook guidance were read; the supplied question and files provide the required compact business context without external discovery. User-requested CSV and Markdown packaging overrides generic inline chart and report offers.

## Source authority and preparation

`source_treatment.json` records byte hashes and measurements, with `profile_inputs.py` as the profiling code. Policy, stock and deliveries are assumed scenario facts for calculating orders. Internally their supplied Provenance labels are synthetic and are preserved truthfully; no real-stock verification is claimed. The sales source's approved original hash differs from released bytes because it is a development partition. The released sales hash is 48238e68f578f73efdbb8db3893cdfc55a0162f16e2e1690e5b2676eeeaaa0b8. No original or withheld file was sought.

17,406 transaction rows become 17,364 after 42 exact duplicates are removed. Remaining transaction identifiers are unique. Eight nonpositive-quantity rows are invalid for gross outflow and excluded. Status case is normalized. Completed, shipped and returned positive quantities give 15,142 eligible rows and 32,198 units. Cancelled orders are excluded; processing orders have not evidenced fulfillment. Returned positive orders count as gross prior outflow, and return-flagged fulfilled orders remain included. There is no dated, saleable-returns ledger to justify netting returns or increasing usable stock. 135 product names are missing, but each product identifier has exactly one non-null name, allowing reliable filling. Unused demographic/pricing fields have no effect on orders. All sales channels are included because every channel has a supplied store assignment; physical stock consumption by channel is an interpretation, not independently known.

The ledger has transactions somewhere on all 1,430 calendar days from 2022-01-01 through 2025-11-30. Approximately 96.9% of policy-key calendar days have no eligible transaction. We fill these with zero **recorded units** conditional on extract completeness, not zero unconstrained demand. No rows are filled outside the observed calendar span. Every current key appears before the first origin (latest first appearance is 2023-03-21). Opening dates, closures, stockouts and lost sales are unavailable. For primary final training, the 104-week window starts December 4, 2023, after every current key's first recorded appearance. The current catalog is a fixed comparison population; discontinued products outside it are unknown. The routine fails on globally missing dates rather than fabricating coverage.

Stock and policy have one row per each of 336 identical keys. All units are product units. The 126 unique incoming order lines sum exactly to stock on-order quantities; the ledger is marked complete for every key. Order dates are not later than planning; future arrival dates December 1–7 are valid known operating inputs, not future outcomes. Policy effective date and stock snapshot are November 30. Cross-file validation is executable in the routine and independently checked for arithmetic in `audit_checks.py`.

## Prospective comparison and selection

`task_refinement.json` and `evaluation_plan.json` were created before scores. Candidate rationale comes from actual sparsity, rising November totals and repeated aggregate seasonal peaks in sales, plus seven-day review and 1–7-day lead times from policy. Fixed candidates were 84-day recent average, 28-day recent average, Croston SBA intermittent-demand smoothing and a regularized pooled Poisson count regression. The latter pools product/store/month effects with a trend, using 104 weeks, sparse matrices and fixed regularization. No neural model, ensemble, expensive search or policy optimization was used. The customer specified only 'last few months', so no exact incumbent rule can be evaluated. mean84 is an analytical baseline only.

All four methods use the same 336 keys and eight Sunday origins from September 28 through November 16. Only training order dates at or before each origin enter forecasts. Both next-seven-day and per-key 8–14-day coverage totals are scored; all outcomes end November 30. The original metric, baseline, settings and selection rule were retained. Coding corrections for optional names and a pandas Index conversion occurred before scores; timing instrumentation later reran the same comparison without score changes. There were no revised candidate searches. Target sensitivities were predeclared diagnostics and did not change selection.

Primary equal-key/origin coverage MAE (product units): mean84 1.275027; mean28 1.389163; Croston SBA 1.252268; Poisson 1.290251. Minimum primary MAE selects Croston SBA. Reduction from baseline is 1.785% and next-week MAE falls from 0.888331 to 0.881704 (0.746%). This is small, not a proven meaningful business improvement. Croston has lower pooled coverage MAE at all eight origins, but store and sparsity results vary (`comparison_by_store.csv`, `comparison_by_sparsity.csv`). Relative errors are high for these sparse units; coverage absolute error exceeds total recorded units. The selected forecast underpredicts coverage on average by 0.286138 units/key/origin (26.5% of pooled actual units). Minimum MAE is not calibrated availability or a service objective, and supplied safety stock is retained rather than optimized. No availability, purchasing-cost, savings or guarantee claims follow.

Adding processing orders or excluding returned/return-flagged orders leaves the same candidate best at both horizons; exact scores are retained in `target_sensitivity.csv`. This supports ranking stability under those interpretations, not certainty about demand. Individual series are too sparse for reliable individual annual seasonal estimates; aggregate November/December peaks recur in 2022–2024, but a pooled seasonal candidate did not improve primary results. December deployment is outside the scored months; no December forecast superiority is established. Training information boundaries on order dates are respected, but old status changes cannot be reconstructed, so historical status availability is an unresolved replay limitation affecting all candidates. No prices, historical stock or stockout exposure were invented to score costs.

Descriptive selected forecast-minus-actual coverage error quantiles (10/50/90%) are -2.576, 0.439 and 1.125 units. They are pooled observations, not calibrated item-level prediction intervals. Eight overlapping coverage origins and already-inspected development outcomes cannot support independent significance claims or a performance-success gate. No externally predeclared meaningful threshold exists. Untouched outcomes and frozen artifacts would be needed for that gate; withheld December outcomes remain untouched. MAE is the declared proxy for quantity accuracy, not an invented priority between availability and excess inventory.

## Ordering and validation

For each key, expected coverage outflow is the selected daily rate times lead plus review days. Target is that outflow plus supplied safety stock. Deduct usable on-hand and only incoming due after planning and by coverage end. Do not double-count stock on-order and receipt totals. Round any positive deficit up to minimum quantity and then the next order multiple; retain zero when no deficit. New arrival date is planning plus lead days. Lead plus review matches the primary comparison horizon, so there is no primary deployment horizon mismatch. Next-week forecasts are separately shown. Coverage extends through December 8–14 depending on lead; no outcomes there are used.

For timing warnings, consume forecast units before allowing that day's incoming receipts; assume receipt at end of day because intraday timing is absent. Maximum cumulative deficit before end-of-day new arrival gives the integer ceiling shown as potential shortage. This is a forecast-based warning, not measured lost sales or an instruction to buy that amount twice. End-of-day timing is conservative and may overflag morning receipts. Overdue receipts are not treated as usable without evidence and are flagged; late-beyond-coverage receipts are separately flagged. None of those exclusions are needed for the current supplied ledger, which arrives within all applicable coverage periods.

Final list: 875 units, 111 positive orders and 225 zeros. 66 keys have potential pre-receipt shortages; 24 have no eligible recorded units in the latest 84 days, and eight of these have positive orders due to longer history/safety-stock deficits and packs. These stale-demand cases warrant business review. `audit_checks.py` independently reconstructs all 336 Croston rates using explicit nonzero-event interval updates, all order arithmetic and all shortage arithmetic, validates keys, units, integer nonnegative orders, pack and minimum compliance and names, and compares a routine rerun byte-for-byte. Agreement is recorded in `validation_results.json` and `routine_measurements.json`. `result.json` contains exactly the required three keys and actual CSV SHA-256. Forecast columns are rounded for display only; ordering uses full precision.

## Local operation, memory and cost

The selected routine uses Python, pandas and NumPy; scikit-learn and SciPy are imported only by the alternative-model branch and needed for comparison reproduction. No local routine calls a network service or language model. Latest actual CLI measurement is in `routine_measurements.json`: approximately 0.617 seconds wall time including process startup/imports, around 0.086 seconds preparation, 0.00184 seconds Croston fit and 0.000017 seconds forecast expansion; ordering/output accounts for most remaining time. The 1,430-by-336 daily matrix occupies 3,843,840 bytes; the 104-by-336 training matrix occupies 279,552 bytes. The routine OS high-water RSS is about 176 MB and covers preparation, fitting, inference, retained arrays and imported libraries, providing an upper bound for both fit and inference memory rather than isolated phase peaks. Resource measurement cannot attribute swap. An initial process-tree observer was sandbox-denied and replaced by resource counters. Full candidate comparison took approximately 1.83 seconds and under 0.3 GB peak RSS; detailed fit/inference timings are retained for all candidates.

These observed workloads leave substantial room for an operating system on an ordinary CPU-only 8 GB computer. This is a compatibility assessment, not a speed benchmark on 8 GB hardware, and larger future catalogs/history require monitoring. Data preparation is roughly linear in transactions plus days times keys; selected fitting is linear in 104 weeks times keys and retained model state is only two smoothed values per series plus forecast arrays. Current host RAM capacity is not assumed. Daily panel creation can dominate memory at larger scale; no claim of unlimited scale is made.

Routine runs have no paid model/API dependency and no model-service fee. Existing computer, electricity or optional hosting costs remain; no rates or energy measurements justify a dollar estimate. AI-assisted analysis/build costs are separate and cannot be estimated from unavailable model usage and applicable pricing. Local calculation seconds are not the elapsed time of this entire AI-assisted investigation. No schedule, upload or integration is installed. `routine_usage.md` explains optional scheduling of the local command with ChatGPT help preparing settings; routine execution does not require another AI call.
