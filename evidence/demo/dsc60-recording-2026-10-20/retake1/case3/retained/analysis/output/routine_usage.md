Run from the folder containing `restock_routine.py` with Python 3, pandas and NumPy on macOS or Linux (including Windows through WSL). See `routine_measurements.json` for the exact recorded dependency versions (pandas 2.3.3 and NumPy 2.3.5).

```sh
python restock_routine.py --sales-path ../released/input-4.csv --stock-path ../released/input-3.csv --deliveries-path ../released/input-2.csv --policy-path ../released/input-1.csv --planning-date 2025-11-30 --output-path next_order.csv
```

Change each path, the planning date and the output filename for a subsequent weekly run with newly supplied files. The example uses the released filenames: transactions are input-4, stock input-3, deliveries input-2 and policy input-1. The output must be a new file; source files are never overwritten. This demo was run only for November 30, 2025. No December sales were read or tested.

Required schemas are validated in `load_inputs` in the program. Dates must use YYYY-MM-DD. Stock must be a complete, unique product/store snapshot for the planning date; policies must already be effective, with one row per product/store. The incoming ledger must be complete, in product units, and match the stock's open-order totals. Future incoming due dates are allowed when the order is already known. Overdue incoming lines, missing whole-ledger dates, stale or future sales, missing required values, conflicting identifiers and incompatible units stop the run rather than invent quantities. At least 182 calendar days of sales history are required. A consistently truncated or omitted local store feed cannot be detected from these validations alone.

The frozen calculation averages the last 182 calendar days of positive completed/shipped recorded sales for each product at a store. It subtracts usable stock and incoming units due within supplier lead time plus review days, adds the supplied safety stock, and rounds any remaining need upward to the minimum and pack multiple. Returns do not add usable stock. Missing individual entries mean no recorded sales, conditional on continuous capture. Expected incoming receipts and new orders are treated as arriving at the end of the due day for timing flags. The daily average estimates cumulative sales, not the exact day of an individual purchase. Timing quantities are fractional expectations; order quantities are whole product units. Demand after the next week is included to cover the supplier waiting time plus review period.

The CSV includes zero orders and flags possible gaps before the new delivery; incoming deliveries can make an order unnecessary even when there is an earlier gap. Review the flagged lines first. Policy buffers and pack rounding can produce orders much larger than predicted sales.

No language model, paid model service, network connection, scheduler or upload integration is needed by the command. It can be scheduled on the user's computer with the operating system's scheduler; ChatGPT can help prepare the command and scheduler setup. No schedule has been installed. A scheduler would need to supply updated file paths, a matching stock date and a fresh output filename each week; the program does not fetch files automatically.
