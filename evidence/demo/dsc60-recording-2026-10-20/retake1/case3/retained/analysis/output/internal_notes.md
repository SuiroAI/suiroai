This is an authorized scenario calculation as of 2025-11-30, not independently verified real-world inventory. Operational CSV provenance literally says synthetic; retained unchanged in source evidence. Sales bytes are the released development partition and differ from the supplied original-source hash. Only the four released CSVs were read. No December 2025 transactions, raw original or other local business files were accessed. The customer's current method was explicitly unavailable; neither recent-average candidate is claimed to reproduce it.

`task_refinement.json` and `evaluation_plan.json` were written before `analysis.py` generated scores. Version 1 was the only candidate comparison plan; no tuning loop, model addition or metric change followed the scores. Development evidence was already inspected in the project. Independent performance-success readiness is false: no externally predeclared meaningful improvement threshold or untouched evaluation exists. Recommendation usefulness does not close this separate gate.

# Source preparation

17,406 raw transaction rows become 17,364 after 42 identical duplicate rows are removed. Each duplicate identifier has identical full-row content; no conflicting identifiers remain. Repeated order identifiers are retained because they need not be duplicate transaction lines. Positive completed/shipped rows after status normalization define a frozen-label fulfilled-sales proxy: 13,602 rows and 29,043 units. Five zero and three negative raw quantities are invalid as positive demand; their full status/flag distribution is retained. Cancelled and processing rows do not establish fulfillment. Returned-only statuses are excluded rather than assuming current reusable stock or a return date; return-flagged completed/shipped lines count as original gross fulfillment. This is not a claim to identify all gross demand or net retained sales. The alternate target sensitivity below includes returned-only positive lines to expose this ambiguity. No negative or return units are credited to usable stock.

Dates span all 1,430 calendar days from 2022-01-01 through 2025-11-30 with some transaction every day globally. There is no missing whole-ledger day. There is no supplied store-opening calendar or per-store feed-completeness marker. Calendar entries without a transaction are zeros of the *recorded-event target*, conditional on continuous capture, not evidence that a store was open, that no stockout occurred or that latent demand was zero. Only 2.79% of pair-days and 17.69% of pair-weeks have positive target units. All 336 policy pairs have history, but fixed present-day assortment could cause survivorship bias. First/last pair events and store date counts are retained. No demand is imputed before an unobserved launch, and no launch dates are fabricated; the complete calendar is an explicit modeling assumption. The routine stops on a global calendar gap. Local store feed omissions remain undetectable.

Twenty-eight product identifiers each map to exactly one non-null product name, enabling reliable name recovery for rows with missing descriptions. The 336 stock and policy keys match exactly; all units are product units. All 126 incoming lines have unique identifiers, known order dates and future due dates. Incoming totals equal all stock on-order totals (927 units); completeness flags are true. On-order quantities are counted once, from the dated ledger, not added again from inventory. Stock is 2,869 units. Historical transaction inventory fields are not a current snapshot or evidence of historical lost sales and are not used.

Historical status transition times are absent. All comparisons therefore use final supplied labels under the same rule; they cannot demonstrate what a live forecast could have known about old order fulfillment at the time. No final status is used as a future-known predictor. Quantities, status exclusions, returns and day-absence ambiguity restrict conclusions to recorded-unit prediction, not service levels or financial savings.

# Candidate rationale and results

The sparse panel supports a transparent recent 56-day mean, a less noisy 182-day mean, and a lightweight pooled count regression. Source observations in `source_treatment.json` and implementations in `restock_routine.py` motivate this set. Poisson regression shares calendar-month effects across products at stores, retains separate pair intercepts and includes a linear time trend with fixed regularization. Weekly full training windows avoid fitting hundreds of thousands of daily rows. Its monthly end-date features approximate weekly exposure; prediction divides weekly expected units by seven. It does not have product-specific seasonal interactions; this is a bounded candidate, not an exhaustive model-family verdict.

Equal-weight pooled seven-day cumulative-unit MAE, predeclared as primary, was 0.835286 for mean56, 0.805904 for mean182 and 0.873470 for Poisson. The selected mean182 lowers this by 3.52% versus the analytical baseline. Coverage-horizon MAE (supplier lead plus review, 8–14 days) was 1.219640, 1.157936 and 1.242228 respectively, so there is no ranking conflict between deployment and selection horizons. Mean182 wins 7/8 origins and 8/12 stores at seven days; it does not dominate every segment. It underpredicts on average: seven-day signed error -0.167196 and coverage error -0.298929 units per pair-origin. MAE weights all units equally, does not assign stockout penalties, and sparse zero periods influence selection. Without an availability/excess priority or cost function, no policy or model cost optimum is asserted.

Aggregate positive fulfilled units in October/November were 667/793 (2022), 589/906 (2023), 628/909 (2024), 795/1,022 (2025). November is stronger each year; monthly duration-adjusted November/October ratios are approximately 1.23, 1.59, 1.50 and 1.33. Recurrence motivated the seasonal candidate, but its measured errors were worse on this common development comparison. The selected mean does not explicitly anticipate holiday peaks; increasing demand by 25% is a limited scenario rather than a validated holiday forecast. No repeated search was used to obtain a gain. Larger hypothetical seasonal increases are not excluded.

The selected model's historical signed-error 10th/50th/90th percentiles are -1.5769/0.2692/0.7692 units for seven days and -2.5385/0.3077/1.0714 for coverage. These pool sparse, heterogeneous outcomes; they are descriptive errors, not calibrated per-row prediction intervals. Overlapping coverage windows are not independent observations. No confidence interval for model superiority or stockout probability is claimed.

# Order arithmetic and useful exceptions

For each row, target = mean daily fulfilled units × (lead days + review days) + supplied safety stock. Subtract usable on-hand and inbound due by coverage end. If residual is positive, raise to at least the minimum and round up to a pack multiple; otherwise order zero. This is an analyst interpretation of the supplied parameters as a periodic order-up-to calculation; no exact incumbent rule was supplied. Orders are product units, not packs. No invented optimization or cost weights are applied.

The result is 868 units on 109 rows and 227 zero-order rows. Safety stock totals 1,544 units while expected coverage demand totals approximately 259 units; policy buffers and pack rounding largely drive these recommendations. Sixty-five pre-new-receipt gaps are flagged, including 12 rows with no new order because a later inbound covers the target. All flagged rows have zero usable stock. Their maximum cumulative expected gaps are only 0.0055–0.9560 units; they are nonzero expectations under intermittent demand, not certain fractional sales or urgency rankings. The receipt-day convention is conservative end of day for inbound and new supply; assess a faster receipt or transfer where a gap matters. This calculation does not authorize actually placing orders or transferring stock.

Demand at 75%/125% of forecast yields 863/875 units, with 5/7 row quantities changing; these are scenarios, not probability bounds. Removing return-flagged completed/shipped rows removes 37 units from the 182-day target and changes no order quantities. Including positive returned-only status as original fulfillment instead yields 875 units, three changed order rows and 111 positive-order rows, retaining all 65 timing flags; this target interpretation sensitivity is in `additional_sensitivities.json` and does not rerank models. The operational fixed buffers limit sensitivity here but do not guarantee adequate service or eliminate excess-stock risk.

# Workload, cost and validation

The selected model needs only pandas/NumPy and Python standard library. It retains 336 rates (2,688 bytes), a 3,843,840-byte daily array and a 37,632-byte prediction array. Full data preparation is included in measurement, with approximately 26.3 MB deep transaction-frame memory; interpreter, libraries, copies and allocations explain the higher process RSS. `routine_measurements.json` records a single cold-process CLI invocation: about 0.664 seconds wall time and 172,883,968 bytes maximum process RSS, including imports, reading, fitting, inference, orders and CSV write. Fitting was about 0.00037 seconds and inference 0.00010 seconds; individual phase high-water RSS is cumulative and does not isolate allocations. These tiny phases are not reliable comparative microbenchmarks. No assumption is made about the host's RAM capacity. At this dataset size the measured workload fits CPU-only 8 GB RAM with substantial operating-system headroom. Growth needs remeasurement, especially retained transaction history; no universal scalability claim is made.

The bounded candidate comparison including all preprocessing, eight origins and three candidates took about 1.18 seconds, peak process RSS approximately 335 MB, with Poisson fitting/inference timings per origin recorded. Its inference covers all pairs for 14 days, fitting at most 52,416 weekly rows with sparse features; no heavy ensemble, neural model, paid API or GPU was used. This comparison compute measurement is also separate from AI-assisted analysis/build elapsed time.

The routine does not need any paid model service. Existing computer, power or optional hosting have ongoing costs. Numeric currency estimates would require energy or hosting usage and applicable rates, which are not available. CSV read-back matches the selected output.
