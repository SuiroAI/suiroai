Methods and assumptions

Preparation: remove 42 exact duplicate rows, leaving 17,364 unique transaction lines with unique Transaction_ID values. Repeated Order_ID values are retained as possible multi-line orders; identifiers alone do not justify removing them. Normalize status case/whitespace. Exclude eight nonpositive-quantity rows (five zero, three negative) as quantity anomalies rather than infer return signs. No other quantity outlier cap is applied; positive quantities range from 1 to 11.

Demand definition: use positive quantities with Completed, Shipped or Returned status, including lines flagged returned exactly once. This yields 15,142 lines and 32,198 units. Cancelled and Processing lines are excluded as unfulfilled demand. Return_Flag overlaps status: 218 completed/shipped rows are flagged Yes, and all 1,540 Returned rows are flagged Yes; flags also occur on excluded statuses. Customer, price and financial anomalies do not enter the unit calculation. All sales channels are assigned to their supplied Store_ID; physical fulfillment attribution is assumed.

Forecast choice: compare 11 fixed candidates using 28/90/180/365-day windows, local rates, product/store pooled rates, 50% blends, and historical seasonal adjustments. At each monthly origin, use only order dates through the previous month end; seasonal factors use completed same-month observations in earlier years. Score all 336 pairs with WAPE = sum absolute forecast errors / sum recorded eligible units, and bias = total forecast / total recorded units − 1. Missing pair records contribute zero recorded units to this scoring target; that does not assert zero underlying demand.

Selected rate: use the 90-day window September 2–November 30, 2025 (2,612 eligible recorded units). For product p and store s, daily rate = [network units for p × network units for s / network total units] / 90. This preserves the observed network total rate while pooling sparse pairs. No seasonal uplift is in the baseline. The historical December uplift sensitivity is 1.254×, the median of December daily units divided by the preceding 90-day daily units in 2022–2024; it suggests 884 ordered units.

Restocking rule: protection horizon H = supplier lead time L + seven-day review period (H = 8–14 calendar days). Target stock = ceil(daily rate × H + supplied safety stock). Credited inbound = open quantities due by November 30 + H. Gap = max(0, target − usable on-hand − credited inbound). If gap > 0, order = order multiple × ceil(max(gap, minimum order) / order multiple); otherwise order = 0. On_Order_Qty is reconciled against the inbound ledger and never added a second time. All 927 inbound units are eligible in the baseline.

Timing rule: project current on-hand plus dated existing receipts minus constant daily demand through H. A new standard order is assumed to arrive at the start of day L. The displayed pre-arrival shortfall is the maximum forecast deficit through day L−1, excluding the new order. An L = 1 order has no pre-arrival full day. The first-gap field in the CSV separately describes the horizon projection without any new order. No automatic transfer or expedition is executed.

Limitations that change the decision

  • Synthetic operating inputs: all 336 stock rows, 126 inbound rows and 336 policy rows have Provenance = synthetic. Their internally consistent balances support an illustration, not a verified inventory commitment or an optimized service-level policy. Do not issue purchase orders from this output alone.
  • Unknown demand exposure: the released sales partition contains at least one record on every network calendar date from January 1, 2022 through November 30, 2025, but this does not establish extraction completeness, store opening days, assortment availability or stockout-free observation. Missing product/store dates remain unobserved demand; no synthetic daily zero-demand rows were created. Rates divide recorded units by calendar days as an explicit continuous-exposure planning assumption. The store-active-date scenario raises exposure-adjusted rates and orders to 888 units, but it is not a statistical bound and may itself be biased upward. Unknown lost sales or partition completeness could have a larger, unquantifiable effect.
  • Sparse local evidence: 193 of 336 pairs have at most three eligible transaction lines in the last 90 days, including 21 with none. Seven recommended pairs have no local eligible line in that window. The selected pooled model borrows product totals and store totals across the network; it assumes comparable product mix across stores and can miss local preferences, assortment restrictions and intermittent demand. A zero observed count is not treated as proof of no demand.
  • Weak predictive resolution: the selected model has 90.39% weighted absolute percentage error and −11.16% aggregate bias in six monthly development folds (June–November 2025). The near-tied 365-day blend has 90.40% error and suggests 876 units. Monthly fold scoring is coarser than the 8–14-day ordering horizon. Candidate selection and performance use the same development folds; there is no untouched validation set, December evaluation, independent verification or achieved service-level estimate. Use scenario stability as a policy observation, not proof of forecast accuracy.
  • Statuses have no change timestamps. This is a retrospective reconstruction using supplied final statuses attached to pre-cutoff order dates; historical status knowledge at each backtest origin cannot be established. December sales were neither accessed nor inferred. Operational due dates in December are accepted as known as-of inputs, not withheld sales outcomes.
  • Returns record demand but do not prove stock recovery. Returned orders and return-flagged fulfilled lines count once in gross historical demand; 3,589 of the 32,198 eligible units are flagged returned. No return quantity, return date or usable disposition is supplied, so returns are never added to current stock. Excluding all flagged returns changes the illustrative order to 876 units, but is not a measured net inventory-flow estimate.
  • Availability and timing are simplified. Usable_On_Hand_Qty is assumed available without another reservation deduction; On_Order_Qty is assumed open, not already received, because its complete-ledger indicator is true and totals reconcile exactly. There are no PO status, receipt, allocation or reservation details to verify that interpretation. Processing and cancelled sales do not establish current commitments, so no historical processing balance is deducted as backlog. No capacity, shelf life, transfer feasibility or order cost is modeled.
  • Calendar-day lead times, day-start receipt availability and an end-of-day November 30 snapshot are assumptions. Demand is spread evenly across each day. Forecast-negative projected balances flag possible timing gaps, not observed stockouts or precise expedite quantities. A seven-day inbound delay increases timing flags from 52 to 56 even though rounded order units stay at 877. A demand spike, late delivery within a day or unavailable store could alter the result.

Source and consistency checks

All dates parse; order years match date years; no sales order date is after the cutoff. Stock/policy keys are unique and both contain the complete 28 products × 12 stores. All sales and inbound keys match that universe, and joins are one-to-one where required.

Stock/policy dates equal November 30. All operational units are product_unit; stock is nonnegative, inbound quantities and order constraints are positive, safety stock is nonnegative, and all open-order completeness indicators are true. Inbound PO-line identifiers are unique, order dates are November 24–30, arrivals are December 1–7 and never precede the PO date. There are zero product/store mismatches between inbound sums and On_Order_Qty.

Product names are missing on 135 raw sales rows; labels are filled from the unique nonmissing name associated with the same Product_ID, without using customer fields. Transaction Inventory_Level has 189 missing raw values and no reliable snapshot semantics; it is excluded in favor of the supplied dated usable-stock file.

Internal code checks reconcile joins, balances, order rounding, table counts and source-byte hashes. These checks share the analysis inputs and do not establish independent numerical or claim verification. No browser rendering inspection was available. The requested static HTML format takes precedence over the plugin’s interactive app runtime.
